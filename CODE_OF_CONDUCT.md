# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person on the other end is doing their best with
what they know.

## What is expected

Most of what happens here is technical disagreement, and technical disagreement
is the point — `CONTRIBUTING.md` names the thing most worth arguing about. Argue
about the work.

- Say what you think is wrong and why, about the change rather than the person.
- Take "I was wrong" as a normal sentence, from anybody, including yourself.
- Assume good faith in a report you find confusing. Somebody who has just hit a
  bug knows less about this codebase than you do; that is the situation, not a
  failing.
- English or Turkish, whichever you are comfortable in. Neither is anybody's
  test to pass, and a report in imperfect English is worth more than one nobody
  wrote.

## What is not

Harassment, personal attacks, sustained disruption, or unwelcome attention of
any kind. Publishing somebody's private information. Any of it in an issue, a
pull request, a commit message, or a review.

## If something happens

Report it to Furkan Beydemir through
[a private security advisory](https://github.com/beydemirfurkan/todox/security/advisories/new),
which is the only private channel this repository has. It is meant for
vulnerabilities and works just as well for this.

You will get a reply. What happens after depends on what happened: a word, an
edit, a locked thread, or a block. This project is maintained by one person, so
there is no committee and no appeals process — which is worth saying plainly
rather than implying otherwise.

## Scope

Anywhere this project is what you are there for: the repository, its issues and
pull requests, and anywhere somebody is representing it.

## Where this came from

The [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org) is the usual
answer here and is a good document. This is shorter and written in this
project's own voice on purpose, because a policy nobody reads protects nobody —
the same reason the rules in `CONTRIBUTING.md` carry the failure that produced
them rather than being a list.
